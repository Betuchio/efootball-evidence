EVIDENCE 01

Card stats are a lie — your players are weaker than you think

Critical

The game maintains two completely separate stat systems: one is what you see on the player card when you buy or train them, the other is what actually operates during the match. They are different values, and you are never told this.

How it works

Inside the game's code, two variables exist for every player attribute: ability_base stores the values printed on the card (e.g., Speed = 95). ability_match stores the actual values used during gameplay (which can be 78, 65, or anywhere lower).

At match start, the game calculates ability_match from ability_base by applying multiple modifiers: Equalization (team balancing), Condition (form), MatchGageAdjustment (real-time speed nerfs), and others. The result is what your player actually does on the pitch.

!
You bought a card that says Speed = 95. In an Equalization-active match, that same player runs at Speed ≈ 75. He is not the player you paid for — and the game never shows you the modified number.
How you feel it
  • Your 95-speed striker gets caught from behind by a 75-speed defender for no obvious reason
  • A 92-rated playmaker passes like an 80-rated player — short passes go astray, lofted balls land wrong
  • Players seem 'lethargic' or 'half-asleep' even when on perfect Condition

EVIDENCE 02

15+ parameters secretly modify player speed in real-time

Critical

MatchGageAdjustment is a system inside the game that modifies player speed, pass speed, intercept speed, sliding speed, and every aspect of movement — in real-time, while the match is running.

What gets modified
adjustSpeed // Base speed multiplier gageAdjustMaxSpeedMinus // Force max speed DOWN gageAdjustMaxSpeedPlus // Force max speed UP gageAdjustSpeedIntercept // Intercept reaction speed adjustDashSpeedRate // Sprint speed rate adjustPassGetSpeed // Pass reception speed adjustMoveSpeedMax // Maximum movement speed adjustPassSpeedMax // Maximum pass speed adjustSlideMoveSpeed // Sliding tackle speed

Each parameter has both a 'minus' (slow down) and 'plus' (speed up) variant. The game can apply them asymmetrically — slow down YOUR players while speeding up the opponent's, in the same match, in real-time.

The smoking gun

The game has a debug log line that proves this is actively used:

[adjustSp] SpChange: %.2f(%.2f / %d)

This line literally says 'speed change' — and Konami's developers wrote it to monitor the system in their internal builds. It is real, it runs, and it logs every adjustment.

⚠
This is why your team feels 'slow' for 10 minutes, then 'fast' again. The system is pulsing — applying nerfs and buffs based on match state, score, possession, and other secret triggers.

EVIDENCE 03

Equalization — Konami's official 'leveling' system

Critical

Konami themselves named this system "Equalization". It is a deliberate mechanism that weakens stronger teams and strengthens weaker ones, eliminating the advantage you paid for.

What it touches
  • Player speed and acceleration — your fast players become slower, theirs become faster
  • Pass success rate — your passes go astray more often, theirs find their target
  • Shot accuracy and finishing — your shots go wide, their shots find corners
  • Defensive reactions — your defenders react slower, their attackers react faster
  • Goalkeeper positioning — your GK 'misreads', their GK is in the perfect spot
Where it activates

m_enableEqualization is a flag that can be turned ON or OFF. In AI Division and most online modes, this flag is permanently ON and the player has no option to disable it. In Custom Match / Friendly modes, the flag can be toggled — meaning Konami knows the system makes matches less fair, and chose to disable that knob for you.

i
The most important word is "Equalization" itself. Konami didn't try to hide it with a vague name. They called it what it is: forced equality. Two teams, no matter how different in quality, will play closer than they should. The 'good vs. bad' result becomes 'random'.

EVIDENCE 05

Your shot's outcome is decided before you take it

Critical

This is the most disturbing finding. The function CheckFutureShootFailedLevel literally translates to "check the failure level of a FUTURE shot". The game decides whether your shot will succeed or fail before the ball leaves your player's foot.

How a shot really works in eFootball
  • You press the shoot button
  • Before the animation plays, the game runs CheckFutureShootFailedLevel
  • It outputs a result: SUCCESS, FAIL, or LINE_OUT (off-target)
  • The animation is then chosen to match the predetermined outcome
  • You see your player 'mishit' the ball — but the mishit was scripted, not physical
Pass success is identical

The same logic applies to passes. pass_success_rate appears 8 times in the binary, and pk_success_rate (penalty kicks) appears 4 times. Each pass is rolled against a probability table — your aim, timing, and skill matter less than the dice the system rolls behind the scenes.

!
You did not 'miss the open net'. The system chose for you to miss it. The animation that played afterwards was the artistic cover for a decision that had already been made.

EVIDENCE 06

Goalkeepers are deliberately delayed — 12 different ways

High

There are two distinct C++ classes for goalkeeper saves: ActionKeeperBlock (normal save) and ActionKeeperBlockLate — a separate class, written specifically to make the keeper react late. This is not a bug. A Konami engineer wrote, named, and shipped this code intentionally.

12 calibrated variants

The delayed-save behavior is calibrated for three goalkeeper styles (Classical, Normal, Offensive) and two distance categories (NEAR, FAR). For each combination, there are also "OK" variants — meaning the system tracks delayed saves that still succeeded. This is a complete simulation of artificial reaction failure.

GK_BLOCKLATE_CLASSICAL / CLASSICAL_NEAR GK_BLOCKLATE_NORMAL / NORMAL_NEAR GK_BLOCKLATE_OFFENSE / OFFENSE_NEAR // + OK variants for each (delayed but still saved)
!
When a 90-rated goalkeeper 'inexplicably' fails to react to a routine shot, this code is why. The system silently switched from ActionKeeperBlock to ActionKeeperBlockLate — same animation, different frame timing, different result.

EVIDENCE 07

Fouls are not based on physics — they're rolled from a probability table

High

In a normal football game, a foul happens because of physical contact. In eFootball, a foul happens because a probability roll succeeded. The probability is set by a lookup table organized by player rating bands. The contact animation is decoration.

The foul probability table
FOUL_SLIDING_DRIBBLE_PARAMTER_40_49 FOUL_SLIDING_DRIBBLE_PARAMTER_50_59 FOUL_SLIDING_DRIBBLE_PARAMTER_60_69 FOUL_SLIDING_DRIBBLE_PARAMTER_70_79 FOUL_SLIDING_DRIBBLE_PARAMTER_80_89 FOUL_SLIDING_DRIBBLE_PARAMTER_90_99 FOUL_SLIDING_DRIBBLE_PARAMTER_100_109 FOUL_SLIDING_DRIBBLE_PARAMTER_110_120 // + _HAVE_SKILL variant for each band
After the system 'sees' a foul, it can cancel it
  • FOUL_JUDGE_CANCEL — the system identified a foul, then cancelled the call
  • FOUL_DEMO_CANCEL — foul replay also cancelled — the player won't even see what happened
  • DUMMY_INTENTIONAL_FOUL — the system can create a fake foul that didn't happen in physics
⚠
You were brought down in the box. The replay shows clear contact. No penalty is given. You blame the referee. But the binary says: FOUL_JUDGE ran, returned TRUE (it WAS a foul), and then FOUL_JUDGE_CANCEL ran and overrode the decision. The referee saw it — the system ordered him to ignore it.

EVIDENCE 12

How to fight back — 13 weak points found in the binary

Counter-strategy

The system has weak points. Every tip below is backed by code found inside the game's executable. These are not guesses — they are exploitable design patterns the system itself reveals.

01 · STAMINA — the scripting amplifier

The binary tracks pass/shot/cross quality at exact stamina percentages: 20-15%, 15-10%, 10-5%, 5-0%. As stamina drops, the scripting effect compounds. Substitute fatigued players at 60-65 minutes. Never let stamina drop below 20% — that's when the system kicks hardest.

<

02 · Confuse the AI — change tactics every 15 minutes

The variable adaptabilityRate proves the AI learns your playing style as the match progresses. marginPredictionFrameAdjust shows it predicts your next move. Switch tactics with the D-pad every 15-20 minutes. Cycle between offensive, balanced, and defensive. Be unpredictable — the predictor breaks when you stop being predictable.

<

03 · Manual passing partially escapes the script

Variables manualPassGetRate, manualPassSpeedBase, manualPassAngleYBase prove that manual passes use a completely separate code path from assisted passes. The system's pass_success_rate manipulation has less grip on your direct input. Switch to PA1 or full manual passing. Your accuracy stops being a die roll and starts being your actual skill again.

<

04 · Don't build a big lead too quickly

The Comeback system has its own animation, sound effect, and C++ class — it is a scripted dramatic mechanism. After scoring 2-0, slow down. Hold possession. Don't attack aggressively. The system needs a "dramatic" trigger to activate full rubber-banding — don't give it one. A patient 2-0 lead held for 30 minutes is safer than a flashy 4-0 lead at the 60th minute.

<

05 · Don't put everyone on BEST condition

The variable condition_cpu_level directly links your team's condition to AI difficulty. All players on BEST = AI plays at LEGEND_PLUS3. Mix GOOD and NORMAL form players. Put a few lower-form players on the bench so they don't inflate the team's "effective overall". You want to be slightly under the threshold that triggers harder AI tiers.

<

06 · Break win streaks deliberately

After 3-4 wins, the streak counter NUM_WIN_STREAK likely triggers Equalization in your next match. Take a break or play a different mode. Switch from AI Division to Friendly. Switch from online to AI matches. The streak counter may be session-based, daily-based, or server-stored — but it definitely tracks you. Don't let it get to a number that hurts you.

<

07 · Equalization can be disabled in some modes

Variables ERoomSettingsKind::EQUALIZATION and GetSelectableRoomSettingsEqualization prove that Equalization is a room-level setting. In Custom Match / Friendly modes, check the room options — there may be a toggle to turn it off. If found, your fully-built squad gets to play as it should — at full power.

<

08 · Vary your shooting — break the prediction model

CheckFutureShootFailedLevel pre-determines outcomes, but marginPredictionFrameAdjust proves the AI uses a prediction model. Different shot types likely use different prediction tables. Mix it up: finesse shots, chip shots, low driven shots, weak-foot shots, shots from tight angles. Don't become the player who always power-shoots to the far corner — that's the easiest pattern to script against.

<

09 · Use a wired connection — asymmetric input delay exists

AsymmetricInputDelayModeEnable, AsymmetricInputDelayWifiEnable, AsymmetricInputDelayWirelessWeight — the game applies different input delays to different players. WiFi connections receive extra weight. AsymmetricInputDelayTurnSuddenChangeEnable means the delay can change suddenly mid-match. Use Ethernet whenever possible. WiFi adds artificial lag on top of any scripting that's also active.

<

10 · Score early — there's a "gimmick window" at kickoff

GimmickMatchStartWindow and GimmickMatchStartWindowEnd reveal a special boost window at match start. initialBoosterId shows initial boosters get applied. Attack immediately after kickoff. The system hasn't calibrated to the match flow yet, and the gimmick window may give you a brief, exploitable advantage. Don't play passive in minute 1 — it's the most uncontested offensive minute of the entire match.

<

11 · Don't spam cursor switching — it's tracked

CURSOR_CHANGE_REPEATEDLY_2/3/4_OR_MORE shows the game counts how rapidly you switch players. CURSOR_CHANGE_OPTIMUM means the system can override your manual selection. RetryAuto_DIFFERENT_CURSOR_TARGET picks a different player than you wanted. Switch deliberately, not frantically. Spamming makes the system "help" you with worse selections than you would have made yourself.

<

12 · Vary set pieces — effectiveness decays

setPlayDeclineRate proves set piece effectiveness degrades over time. DisableBufferingControlInSetPlay shows input buffering changes during set plays. Don't use the same corner kick or free kick routine repeatedly. Change your target, delivery type, runner pattern. Repeated patterns are detected and weakened by the system on purpose.

<

13 · Wet pitch helps you — use it

PITCH_CONDITION_DRY/NORMAL/WET — three pitch states. EPitchEffectType::Ball proves the pitch directly affects ball physics. EPitchEffectType::FootTrail affects player traction. On wet pitch, use ground passes and low driven shots. Aerial play becomes less predictable, which actually works against the prediction system. The wetter the pitch, the worse the AI predicts your moves.
