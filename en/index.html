EVIDENCE 01

Card stats are a lie — your players are weaker than you think

Critical

The game maintains two completely separate stat systems: one is what you see on the player card when you buy or train them, the other is what actually operates during the match. They are different values, and you are never told this.

How it works

Inside the game's code, two variables exist for every player attribute: ability_base stores the values printed on the card (e.g., Speed = 95). ability_match stores the actual values used during gameplay (which can be 78, 65, or anywhere lower).

At match start, the game calculates ability_match from ability_base by applying multiple modifiers: Equalization (team balancing), Condition (form), MatchGageAdjustment (real-time speed nerfs), and others. The result is what your player actually does on the pitch.

!
You bought a card that says Speed = 95. In an Equalization-active match, that same player runs at Speed ≈ 75. He is not the player you paid for — and the game never shows you the modified number.
How you feel it
  • Your 95-speed striker gets caught from behind by a 75-speed defender for no obvious reason
  • A 92-rated playmaker passes like an 80-rated player — short passes go astray, lofted balls land wrong
  • Players seem 'lethargic' or 'half-asleep' even when on perfect Condition
Same player, two values. The lower one is what plays.

EVIDENCE 02

15+ parameters secretly modify player speed in real-time

Critical

MatchGageAdjustment is a system inside the game that modifies player speed, pass speed, intercept speed, sliding speed, and every aspect of movement — in real-time, while the match is running.

What gets modified
adjustSpeed // Base speed multiplier gageAdjustMaxSpeedMinus // Force max speed DOWN gageAdjustMaxSpeedPlus // Force max speed UP gageAdjustSpeedIntercept // Intercept reaction speed adjustDashSpeedRate // Sprint speed rate adjustPassGetSpeed // Pass reception speed adjustMoveSpeedMax // Maximum movement speed adjustPassSpeedMax // Maximum pass speed adjustSlideMoveSpeed // Sliding tackle speed

Each parameter has both a 'minus' (slow down) and 'plus' (speed up) variant. The game can apply them asymmetrically — slow down YOUR players while speeding up the opponent's, in the same match, in real-time.

The smoking gun

The game has a debug log line that proves this is actively used:

[adjustSp] SpChange: %.2f(%.2f / %d)

This line literally says 'speed change' — and Konami's developers wrote it to monitor the system in their internal builds. It is real, it runs, and it logs every adjustment.

⚠
This is why your team feels 'slow' for 10 minutes, then 'fast' again. The system is pulsing — applying nerfs and buffs based on match state, score, possession, and other secret triggers.
In an Equalization-active match, the gap nearly disappears.

EVIDENCE 03

Equalization — Konami's official 'leveling' system

Critical

Konami themselves named this system "Equalization". It is a deliberate mechanism that weakens stronger teams and strengthens weaker ones, eliminating the advantage you paid for.

What it touches
  • Player speed and acceleration — your fast players become slower, theirs become faster
  • Pass success rate — your passes go astray more often, theirs find their target
  • Shot accuracy and finishing — your shots go wide, their shots find corners
  • Defensive reactions — your defenders react slower, their attackers react faster
  • Goalkeeper positioning — your GK 'misreads', their GK is in the perfect spot
Where it activates

m_enableEqualization is a flag that can be turned ON or OFF. In AI Division and most online modes, this flag is permanently ON and the player has no option to disable it. In Custom Match / Friendly modes, the flag can be toggled — meaning Konami knows the system makes matches less fair, and chose to disable that knob for you.

i
The most important word is "Equalization" itself. Konami didn't try to hide it with a vague name. They called it what it is: forced equality. Two teams, no matter how different in quality, will play closer than they should. The 'good vs. bad' result becomes 'random'.
The server sits between you and the result. It can change anything.

Critical

The client cannot refuse — the server's word is law.

EVIDENCE 05

Your shot's outcome is decided before you take it

Critical

This is the most disturbing finding. The function CheckFutureShootFailedLevel literally translates to "check the failure level of a FUTURE shot". The game decides whether your shot will succeed or fail before the ball leaves your player's foot.

How a shot really works in eFootball
  • You press the shoot button
  • Before the animation plays, the game runs CheckFutureShootFailedLevel
  • It outputs a result: SUCCESS, FAIL, or LINE_OUT (off-target)
  • The animation is then chosen to match the predetermined outcome
  • You see your player 'mishit' the ball — but the mishit was scripted, not physical
Pass success is identical

The same logic applies to passes. pass_success_rate appears 8 times in the binary, and pk_success_rate (penalty kicks) appears 4 times. Each pass is rolled against a probability table — your aim, timing, and skill matter less than the dice the system rolls behind the scenes.

You did not 'miss the open net'. The system chose for you to miss it. The animation that played afterwards was the artistic cover for a decision that had already been made.

Fouls are not based on physics — they're rolled from a probability table

High

In a normal football game, a foul happens because of physical contact. In eFootball, a foul happens because a probability roll succeeded. The probability is set by a lookup table organized by player rating bands. The contact animation is decoration.
